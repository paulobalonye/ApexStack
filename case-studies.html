Fintech Payments PCI-DSS

NovaPay: Reduced Cloud Costs by 47% While Scaling to 50M Transactions per Month.

The Challenge

NovaPay, a fast-growing African payment processor, was experiencing rapid transaction growth that was outpacing its cloud infrastructure. Their AWS environment had been provisioned reactively over two years, resulting in over-provisioned EC2 instances, unoptimized database configurations, and a monthly cloud bill that was growing 30% faster than revenue. With plans to expand into three new West African markets, they needed infrastructure that could handle 50M+ monthly transactions while maintaining PCI-DSS compliance and sub-10ms payment processing latency.

Our Approach

ApexStack embedded a team of three cloud architects and two SRE engineers with NovaPay's infrastructure team for a 16-week engagement. We began with a comprehensive infrastructure audit, mapping every workload, data flow, and cost center across their AWS estate.

  • Migrated payment processing workloads from EC2 to AWS EKS with auto-scaling pod configurations tuned for transaction volume patterns
  • Redesigned the database layer with Aurora PostgreSQL read replicas and connection pooling, eliminating the single-database bottleneck
  • Implemented a FinOps framework with real-time cost dashboards, reserved instance planning, and automated rightsizing recommendations
  • Built a PCI-DSS compliant CI/CD pipeline with automated security scanning, secret management via AWS Secrets Manager, and immutable infrastructure deployments
  • Established a multi-region disaster recovery architecture with RPO under 15 minutes and RTO under 30 minutes

Results

Within four months, NovaPay's monthly AWS spend dropped from $380K to $201K while simultaneously scaling to handle 50M+ transactions per month. Payment processing latency improved from 45ms to under 8ms. The new infrastructure passed a full PCI-DSS Level 1 audit on the first attempt, and the FinOps dashboards gave NovaPay's finance team real-time visibility into cost allocation by product line for the first time.

SaaS Platform Engineering DevOps

CloudMetrics: From Monolith to Microservices — 12x Faster Deployments.

The Challenge

CloudMetrics, a B2B observability SaaS platform with 2,000+ enterprise customers, was trapped in a monolithic architecture that made feature development painfully slow. Every code change required a full application deployment, creating a release cycle of once per month with frequent rollbacks. Engineering teams were spending 40% of their time on deployment coordination rather than building product. With Series C funding secured and aggressive growth targets, they needed to ship 10x faster without breaking customer environments.

Our Approach

ApexStack deployed a platform engineering squad of four engineers who worked alongside CloudMetrics' development team for 20 weeks. We designed and executed a phased decomposition strategy that allowed the monolith to be broken apart incrementally without any customer-facing downtime.

  • Decomposed the monolithic application into 14 bounded-context microservices using a strangler fig migration pattern
  • Built a Backstage-powered internal developer portal with self-service environment provisioning, service catalog, and golden path templates
  • Implemented a multi-tenant Kubernetes architecture on GKE with namespace isolation, resource quotas, and tenant-aware auto-scaling
  • Designed a CI/CD pipeline with trunk-based development, feature flags via LaunchDarkly, and canary deployments that roll back automatically on error rate spikes
  • Established service mesh with Istio for inter-service communication, observability, and traffic management

Results

CloudMetrics went from deploying once per month to 12 times per day within five months. Rollback incidents dropped by 94% thanks to canary deployments and automated error detection. Engineering teams reported spending 60% more time on feature development and 40% less on infrastructure coordination. Most critically, the entire migration was completed with zero customer-facing downtime.

Mobility Real-Time Multi-Region

RideFleet: Infrastructure for 3M Daily Rides Across 4 Countries.

The Challenge

RideFleet, a ride-hailing platform operating across Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, and South Africa, was struggling with infrastructure that couldn't keep pace with exponential rider growth. Their matching algorithm was running on a single-region deployment, causing 500ms+ response times during peak hours and frequent service degradation in markets with less reliable network connectivity. Driver assignment failures during surge periods were costing the company an estimated $2M annually in lost rides, and the platform needed to handle 3M+ daily ride requests with sub-second matching.

Our Approach

ApexStack assembled a team of two distributed systems architects and three platform engineers for a 24-week engagement focused on rebuilding RideFleet's core infrastructure for global scale and resilience.

  • Redesigned the ride-matching engine as an event-driven architecture using Amazon Kinesis for real-time geospatial data streaming and processing
  • Deployed multi-region infrastructure across AWS af-south-1, eu-west-1, and me-south-1 with intelligent traffic routing based on rider location
  • Built a custom auto-scaling framework that predicts demand surges using historical ride data and pre-warms compute capacity 15 minutes ahead of peak periods
  • Implemented a geospatial indexing layer using Redis with H3 hexagonal grid cells for O(1) driver proximity lookups
  • Designed a graceful degradation strategy for low-connectivity markets, with offline-capable driver apps and asynchronous ride confirmation queues

Results

RideFleet's platform now processes over 3M daily rides across four countries with ride-matching latency under 200ms, down from 500ms+. The predictive auto-scaling framework eliminated surge-period failures entirely, recovering the estimated $2M in previously lost ride revenue. Multi-region deployment reduced latency for African markets by 65%, and the graceful degradation strategy ensured uninterrupted service in areas with intermittent connectivity. RideFleet has since expanded to two additional markets on the same infrastructure without requiring additional architectural changes.
